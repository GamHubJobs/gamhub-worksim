# Cases: a guide for players

Besides your tasks, real workplace business reaches you as **cases**: a client who is
unhappy, a colleague in another department who needs something, a purchase request,
a meeting to prepare, an incident, and later a team to lead. This guide explains how
they work.

## Where cases come from

A case arrives by **email** from a person: a client, a supplier, a colleague or another
department. It arrives at a natural point in your career, for example after you pass a
task or finish an earlier case. Open it from the email (**Open case**) or from **Cases**
on the dock.

You will never have more than two ordinary cases open at once. Two kinds of case always
arrive straight away, because they come from your own work:

- a follow-up to something you handed over;
- the next stage of a programme you are working on.

## Working on a case

Each case shows:

- who it is from;
- why it matters;
- the evidence: messages, tables and records;
- the deadline, in office time;
- a list of steps.

The steps are things people really do at work:

| Step         | What you do                                                      |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Review**   | Tick every statement about the evidence that applies             |
| **Figure**   | Work out a number from the evidence                              |
| **Decide**   | Choose what to do                                                |
| **Approve**  | Approve, reject or escalate requests (see “Your authority”)      |
| **Assign**   | Share out work by people’s strengths and capacity (leaders)      |
| **Actions**  | Give each agreed action the right owner                          |
| **Reply**    | Write the email or note, covering the points that matter         |
| **Hand-off** | Fill in what another department needs to do its part             |
| **Document** | Prepare a file in Docs or Slides (a meeting update, for example) |

Your answers are saved as you go. When you are ready, choose **Submit**. You have one
attempt, as at work, so check your answers first.

## Results and consequences

Every step is checked. The result explains each mark: what you chose, what was expected,
and why. You need **70%** to pass. Each result also shows an example reply.

Cases have consequences. They are small, they are always explained, and you can recover
from them:

- **Trust** with the person involved.
- **Your professional reputation.**
- **Your department’s spending**, measured against its quarterly budget.
- **The company’s indicators:** client satisfaction, risk and discipline (each out of
  100). You can see these in Cases and on the notice board in the break room.

If a deadline passes before you submit, the case **lapses**. The person is let down,
the consequences apply and the day’s log records what happened. A wrong hand-off
comes back as a follow-up case: the other department cannot do its part until you put
it right.

## Reminders in the office

The office keeps you informed without stopping your work:

- **At your computer**, a reminder appears when a case is due within the hour, or when
  a case meeting is about to start or has started. **Open the case** takes you to it.
- **The team whiteboard** at your desk lists your open cases and how long each has left.
- **The notice board** in the break room shows the company indicators and, once it has
  started, the progress of the Manchester office programme.

## Meetings

Some cases are meetings. The case tells you when and where the meeting is. There are
three parts:

1. **Prepare** before the meeting: the figures and the file you will present.
2. **Join the meeting** at the start time. You can **Work until the meeting starts**, or carry on
   with something else. The meeting takes its time on the office clock.
3. **Follow up** afterwards: decisions, actions and the minutes.

If you do not attend, the meeting and the follow-up steps score nothing.

## Your authority

What you may decide grows with your level. The **Career** app shows your current
authority.

| Level         | You can approve spending of up to |
| ------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 1 (first job) | Nothing: requests go to a manager |
| 2             | £500                              |
| 3             | £2,500                            |
| 4             | £10,000                           |
| 5             | £25,000                           |

Even within your limit, reject anything that breaks policy. Escalate anything above
your limit, or beyond the department’s remaining budget.

**Leading a team** starts at level 4 in every career. Leadership cases ask you to:

- share out work fairly;
- review a colleague’s work;
- settle disagreements;
- give feedback that criticises the work, never the person.

## The Manchester office programme

From level 3, your department takes part in an organisation-wide programme: **Opening
the Manchester office**. Your part has two stages:

1. Deliver your department’s contribution, working with what other departments send.
2. Advise the programme review on a change.

The **programme board** in each programme case shows every department’s workstream.
Colleagues deliver theirs as the days pass.

## Your portfolio

Every case you pass becomes a portfolio item, with a record of the case, your answers
and the result. When you finish both programme stages, they become one **project** in
your portfolio. See [PORTFOLIO_GUIDE.md](PORTFOLIO_GUIDE.md).
