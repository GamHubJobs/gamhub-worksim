# GamHub WorkSim: player handbook

Start here. This page covers everything you need to get going, with links to the
detailed guides. Inside WorkSim, **Help** in the top bar has the same answers in
short.

## What WorkSim is, and what it is not

WorkSim is a **workplace simulation**. You join Cobalt Ridge Consulting in one of six
careers:

- Finance & Administration;
- Accounting;
- IT Help Desk;
- Digital Marketing;
- Data Analysis;
- Software Development.

You do realistic work in working office apps and profession tools, and get clear
feedback on every piece of work.

WorkSim is **not** a job, a course with a certificate, or a qualification. Your
portfolio shows work done in a simulation, and says so on every page and export.

WorkSim is free, needs no account, and keeps your progress **on this device only**.

## Starting

1. Open WorkSim. The start screen explains what it is.
2. **Choose a career.** Each starts at its first level, in its own department, with
   its own manager, colleagues, tasks and tools. You can start the other careers later
   from the **Career** app; each keeps its own progress.
3. Type your first name (what your colleagues will call you) and choose **Start my
   first day**.

## The office

You walk the office yourself (W A S D, the mouse to look, E to use things or talk to
people), or use the **Places** bar and the panel, which walk you there. Going from one
area to another (reception, your desk, the manager's office, the break room) takes a
few minutes of office time. At your desk you sit down and log in before the computer
opens; colleagues are spoken to face to face; coffee is made at the machine and lunch
eaten at the table.

You arrive at reception in a 3D office.

- **Places** (or the blue rings on the floor) take you to your desk, your manager’s
  office or the break room.
- The **panel beside the 3D view** lists everything you can do where you are: talk to
  colleagues, read the whiteboard, have lunch, go home. You never need the 3D view
  itself.
- At your desk, **Use your computer** opens your apps. **Leave desk** goes back to the
  office.
- The day runs in office time. Meetings, breaks and lunch matter, and so does going
  home on time: your energy and stress change with how you work.

### Meetings

When a meeting is on, **Join** (in the panel) walks you to it: your place in the circle
at the whiteboard for the stand-up, your chair in the manager's office for planning and
the one-to-one. Listen while the others speak in turn, give your update, and the meeting
counts. Walk away part way and it does not.

### Talking to people

Everyone you see can be talked to, at work and in town: colleagues, the barista, the
cashier, the trainer, people in the street. Walk up and press **E** ("talk to Leila"),
or click them; hovering shows their name and role. They stop and turn to you, and you
choose what to say. What you can say depends on who they are, and what they say back
depends on their personality, what they are doing (someone in a meeting or mid-workout
will tell you to come back later; two colleagues chatting can be joined), how well they
know you and what happened last time. Friendly conversations build trust a little, once
a day per person; a real favour builds more; rudeness costs trust and is remembered.
Press **Esc** or **Goodbye** to end a conversation. See [People](PEOPLE.md).

### The screen

The 3D world fills the screen. What you can do where you are is in the panel on the left
(Hide folds it away); how you are, and Phone, Map and Menu, are at the bottom right.
Press **Esc** for the pause menu: missions, relationships, your character, inventory,
settings, saving and backups, and the main menu. See [The game screen](GAME_SCREEN.md).

### Getting close to someone

Some of the people you meet (at the café, the gym, the garage, the office) could become
friends, and some of them more. Ask what they love doing, pay a compliment, flirt once
you are friends, and ask them out. They answer as themselves: yes, no, or not yet, and a
no is respected. On the evening of a date, go to the café or the park, walk up to them,
sit down together and talk; what you say, and what you remember about them, decides how
it goes. Once you are dating you can become a couple, invite them round, ask them
to stay, move in together, propose and marry (a wedding in the park, with your friends),
and, if you both want, start a family. Things can go wrong (missed dates add up), and
they can be talked through, or ended, by either of you. Staying single is just as good a
way to play.

Children grow up: a year passes every two game weeks, from a baby to a toddler, a school
child and a teenager, and what you can do together changes with them (a lullaby, a tower
of blocks, homework, a video game). On weekdays school-age children are at school from a
quarter to nine until half past three; at eighteen they are grown up and living away.
If money runs low while you live together, your partner will want to talk about it:
agreeing a budget together brings you closer, brushing it off does not.

In the café and the shop, customers come and go all day: in through the door, to the
counter or round the aisles, and out again. If your takeaway arrives while you are out
in the street, the courier waits at your building's door: walk up to collect it.

### The map

Press **M** for the world map (or Map on the HUD, in the pause menu, or the phone). Click a
place to see what is there and set it as your destination: the route shows on the map and
on the mini-map at the bottom right. You still walk there. See [The map](MAP.md).

### Your phone

Press **P** (or Open your phone) to take out your phone: messages and calls with
friends, contacts, your calendar, a map, your bank account, career, food delivery, a
social feed, a browser, a camera and settings. Your character holds it and stands still
while it is out; press **P** or **Esc** to put it away. See [The phone](PHONE.md).

### Story Mode

Choose **Story Mode** on the first screen, or press **Story** in the top bar, to play a
story told in missions. Every career has its own: "Short Delivery" (Finance &
Administration), "The Halden Account" (Accounting), "Ticket Zero" (IT Help Desk),
"Launch Week" (Digital Marketing), "Signal and Noise" (Data Analysis) and "Release Day"
(Software Development). The
tracker at the top right says what to do now and where. Missions are played in person:
walk up to the person a mission is about and their scene plays, with choices; some need
work on your computer, a meeting, or a visit somewhere in town. What you decide comes
back later. Between missions your time is your own, and everything else in your career
carries on as normal. The Story screen lists the missions (start one, or switch), the
people in your story and the decisions you made.

## Life outside work

After work you go home. The end-of-day summary still comes first; **Go home** takes
you to your flat.

- **Rooms**: kitchen, bathroom, bedroom, living room and the front door. Each lists what
  you can do there, how long it takes and what it costs: cook, order a takeaway or
  groceries, shower, read, watch a series, call a friend, go for a run, meet friends in
  town, and go to bed.
- **A takeaway** comes to the door: order it on your phone (it is paid then), and about
  25 minutes later the doorbell rings. Do something else meanwhile, or wait for it.
  Answer the door and the courier hands you the bag; then eat it at the table. If you do
  not answer within a few minutes, it is left on the doorstep for you to pick up.
- **Needs**: hunger, hygiene, social and fun (high is good), with your energy and
  stress. They fall as time passes; what you do raises them.
- **Sleep**: the alarm rings at 7:00. Going to bed late means less sleep, and less
  energy at work.
- **The morning**: have breakfast, a shower and get dressed, then leave from the front
  door: walk (free, 40 minutes), bus (£2.50, 25 minutes) or taxi (£14, 12 minutes).
  Each shows when you would arrive. Late, hungry, unwashed or in yesterday's clothes:
  it shows at work (energy, stress, reputation), in your day log.
- **Your home in 3D**: you walk about the flat (W A S D, the mouse to look) and do
  things there: cook at the hob and eat at the table, wash at the basin and shower,
  watch TV from the sofa, lie down in bed. The room lists start the same activities.
- **Furniture** (**Furniture shop** in _Your home_): each piece says what it does. Some
  make activities possible (a games console, a study desk for skill XP, a coffee
  machine for morning energy, an exercise mat for yoga), some make them better (a
  sofa and television for series, a fitted kitchen for meals, a good bed for sleep), and
  some make home nicer to come back to (plants, a lamp, a rug: fun falls more slowly).
  Pick a colour before buying, or change it later. A new piece for a place sells the
  old one second-hand.
- **Moving home** (**Move home**): a one-bedroom flat or a terraced house, each nearer
  work (a shorter commute) and with a higher rent, and a one-off cost to move in. Your
  furniture comes with you.
- **Friends and your phone** (**Open your phone**): Leo (from university), Aisha (your
  neighbour), Dan (the running club) and Mei (the book club). Each friendship grows when
  you make time for them and fades if you do not keep in touch for a few days.
  - **Invitations** arrive on most evenings, from the friend you have seen least:
    dinner, the cinema, a run, a quiz night, a drink, an online game. **Accept** and go
    from the **Front door** at the time; **Decline** politely (it costs a little); an
    invitation you ignore is missed, and a promise you do not keep hurts most.
  - **Get in touch**: send a message, call, or invite someone round for dinner (you need
    two meals in the fridge and a table).
- **The neighbourhood** (**Go out into the neighbourhood** at the front door): your
  street in 3D, with people walking about. Click the view, then walk with the arrow keys
  or W, A, S and D (Up and Down walk, Left and Right turn, Shift runs) and press Enter at
  a door; or choose a place from the list, which shows how long the walk is and whether
  it is open. Walking takes time on the clock.
  - Inside each place you see it in 3D (see [LOCATIONS.md](LOCATIONS.md)) and walk your
    character about: **W A S D** move, **Shift** runs, **C** crouches, **Space** jumps,
    drag the mouse or press **Q** and **R** to look around, and the wheel zooms (or use
    **Mouse look**, and Esc to release it). Near something usable, **Press E**: the
    character walks over and does it for real, from a bench press to a week's shopping,
    and it counts when it is finished (moving or pressing E stops it, and nothing is
    given part way). Ringed objects and the list start the same activities. See
    [CHARACTER.md](CHARACTER.md).
  - **Kettle & Crumb** (café, 7:00–22:00): breakfast, coffee and cake, dinner.
  - **Daily Basket** (food shop, 7:00–23:00): a week of groceries for less than having
    them delivered, or a single ready meal.
  - **Forge Fitness** (gym, 6:00–23:00): a workout, a fitness class, and showers in the
    changing rooms.
  - **Millpond Park** (always open): a walk round the pond, a run, a bench.
  - **Home**: go back inside. You can only go to bed at home.
  - **Cog & Kerb** (bikes and car hire, 8:00–20:00): buy a city bike (£240; it sells
    back for £96) or lease a small car (£250 for the first week, then £60 every Monday
    with the rent, until you hand it back).
  - **Go to work** (mornings only): choose how, at the end of the street; the day starts
    when you arrive.
  - Traffic goes up and down the street (it stops for you). With a bike, **Get on your
    bike** makes walking between places twice as quick; your bike waits by your door
    and a leased car is parked outside.
  - In 3D you walk the street like the places (W A S D, the mouse to look). **Press E**
    at a door to go in. **F** gets you on or off your bike, and next to your leased car
    gets you in: W and S to drive and brake, A and D to steer, and F again to park and
    get out.
- **Ways to work** (from the front door or the end of the street), with when you would
  arrive:

  | Way  | Time | Cost                   | Also                                    |
  | ---- | ---- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
  | Walk | 40   | free                   | a little tiring                         |
  | Bike | 20   | free (own a bike)      | fun, less stress, you arrive warm       |
  | Bus  | 25   | £2.50                  |                                         |
  | Car  | 15   | £4.50 fuel and parking | needs a lease; the traffic is stressful |
  | Taxi | 12   | £14                    |                                         |

  A home nearer work takes minutes off every way.

- **Your laptop** (evenings at home): your career outside the office.
  - **Jobs**: four other employers are hiring (a housing trust, a freight company, a
    firm of advisers and the district council), each for a role in your own career at
    your level or one above, with the pay they would offer. They screen on your real
    record: your level, tasks done to standard and your latest review. They reply the
    next evening, with reasons if it is a no. An interview is three questions; answers
    with a real example (what happened, what you did, how it turned out) score best,
    and 4 out of 6 gets an offer, which stands for a week. You stay at your company:
    an offer is something to mention when you ask for a rise.
  - **Pay**: write to your manager asking for 3, 6, 10 or 15% (every four weeks). They
    answer the next evening from your latest review, your reputation and any offer you
    mention. Ask for more than that supports and you get what it does support, or
    nothing; asking for a lot with nothing behind it costs some goodwill. Rises stay
    with you when you are promoted.
  - **Side business**: evening tutoring (£30 to set up), handmade cards and prints
    (£60) or bike tune-ups (£45, needs your own bike). Each evening's work pays, more
    for every three sessions as regulars come. Starting one declares it to your
    employer. More than three evenings a week on top of work is tiring.
  - **WorkSim AI** (evenings and mornings): help with daily life. **Check my budget**
    sorts the week's money (pay, rent, travel, food, the car, the side business), says
    what is due and suggests savings (for example, taxis that the bus would have
    covered). **Plan my evening** puts your lowest needs first and gives the time to
    be in bed for 8 hours' sleep; in the morning it lists what is left to do and when
    to leave. **What should I learn next?** names your least practised skills, what
    the next promotion still needs and what employers would want first. You can also
    type a question. It uses the same engines as WorkSim AI at work: the built-in
    guide (WorkSim's own rules, not an AI model, offline and free) unless you have
    turned on a model there. Every answer says what wrote it, and **Show what WorkSim
    AI is given** lists what would be sent (never your name).

- **Money**: you are paid on Fridays (more as you are promoted) and rent is taken on
  Mondays. The statement lists everything in and out. Going overdrawn is stressful.

## Your computer

Your work computer has a desktop like a Windows 10 PC.

- **Desktop icons** down the left and right: double-click one (or select it and press
  Enter) to open the app. Drag an icon to move it; WorkSim remembers where you put it.
- **Right-click the desktop** for the menu: icon size (**View**), **Sort by** name,
  **Refresh**, **New** (a document, presentation or practice workbook, depending on
  your career), **Display settings** and **Personalize** (background and icon size).
- **Each app opens in a window.** Drag the title bar to move it and the edges to
  resize it; use the buttons at the top right to minimise, maximise (or double-click
  the title bar) and close. Several apps can be open at once; the one you click comes
  to the front.
- **The taskbar** along the bottom: **Start** (every app, A–Z, and settings), **Type
  here to search**, a button per app (a line under it when it is open, a red number
  for new items), and **Leave desk**. Clicking the button of the window in front
  minimises it; clicking again brings it back.
- **The right-hand end of the taskbar** shows you and your XP, the network, your
  energy as a battery, **Help** (?), **Storage & backup**, and the office clock: click
  the clock for your day. The thin strip at the far right is **Show desktop**.
- **Notifications** (a meeting starting, a case arriving) appear in the bottom-right
  corner.

Windows stay as you left them when you leave your desk, and after you close WorkSim.

## WorkSim AI

**WorkSim AI** is the assistant on your work computer (desktop icon, taskbar, Start,
search). It helps with what you have open: plan a task, explain a formula, summarise an
email, list what it asks for, outline a reply, review your writing, and write better
prompts.

- **Modes.** **Learning** explains and gives one step at a time; **Professional** gives
  practical guidance and reviews; **Expert** explains trade-offs and leaves the decision
  to you. The mode changes how much is explained, not how much is done for you.
- **Context.** The panel on the right lists what WorkSim AI knows for the next question
  (the email or task you have open, your role). Untick anything you do not want to
  share. Your name is never shared.
- **Who answers.** Every answer says what wrote it.
  - **Built-in guide** (the default): WorkSim's own rules. Free, offline, **not an AI
    model**, so it answers specific questions only.
  - **A model on your computer**: free. Install [Ollama](https://ollama.com), download a
    model (for example `ollama pull llama3.2`), start it so WorkSim may use it
    (`OLLAMA_ORIGINS=http://localhost:5173 ollama serve`, using WorkSim's address),
    then choose it in WorkSim AI's Settings with the model's name.
  - **A cloud model through the WorkSim AI bridge**: uses your own API key, and the
    provider charges your account per request. Put the key in a file called
    `.env.local` next to `package.json` (for example `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=...`; OpenAI
    and Google also need `OPENAI_MODEL` or `GEMINI_MODEL`), run `npm run ai-bridge`,
    and choose the provider in Settings. The key stays in the bridge.
- **Consent.** Nothing is sent to a model until you tick the consent box in Settings.
  **Show what will be sent** shows the exact context. If the model cannot answer, WorkSim
  says why and the built-in guide answers instead.
- **Inside the apps.** Each app has a **WorkSim AI** button in its toolbar with what it
  can do there:
  - **Mail**: summarise an email, list what it asks you to do, outline a reply; review
    your draft, or improve its wording.
  - **Tasks**: how to approach the task, and which step to do first.
  - **Grid Craft**: explain a formula or an error, suggest a formula for the selected cell,
    and analyse the selected data (totals, unusual values, trends, which chart to use).
  - **Word Craft**: review your writing, summarise, suggest a report structure, rewrite the
    selected text.
  - **Slides**: review the presentation, suggest an outline, help with speaker notes.
  - **Code**: explain the code and the failing tests, and which tests to write.
  - **Help Desk**: summarise the ticket, suggest a resolution from the knowledge base,
    help with the reply.
- **Proposed changes.** When WorkSim AI suggests a formula or rewritten text, it shows it
  first, with **Apply** and **Dismiss**. Nothing in your work changes until you apply
  it, and you can undo it in the app afterwards. In Learning mode, formula help is a
  hint, so you write the formula yourself.
- **Learning to use AI well.** WorkSim AI gives a **prompt tip** in Learning mode when a
  question is vague, and pauses if a question seems to contain personal or secret
  information (an email address, a phone number, a password…): **Edit my question** to
  take it out. Under an AI model's answer, **I have checked this answer** records that
  you verified it.
- **Your manager's expectations.** The first time you use WorkSim AI, your manager sends
  the company's AI policy: you are responsible for what you send, keep personal and
  confidential data out, use only the approved tool, and check every figure.
- **AI cases.** From day 2, three cases from your manager practise AI at work: checking
  an AI-written summary against the data, deciding what may go into an AI tool, and
  writing a prompt that works. They are graded like every case.
- **AI skills.** The Career app's **Using AI at work** panel shows your prompting,
  checking AI answers, protecting data and reviewing AI changes, from evidence. Asking
  more questions does not raise them. The AI cases add to the **Using AI at work**
  skill, which every career has.
- **Limits.** A daily limit (40 requests by default, on this computer) and a time limit
  on each request. The bridge has its own daily limit too.

## Tasks, cases and assessments

- **Tasks** come from your manager by email. Open one in Tasks and choose **Start
  task**. Work through the steps, using the hints, then hand it in. Every check is
  marked and explained. You can improve your work and hand it in again.
- **Cases** are workplace situations with a deadline. Read the evidence, work through
  the steps and submit; a case is answered once, like a real email. See
  [CASES_GUIDE.md](CASES_GUIDE.md).
- **Readiness assessments** (from level 2) are practical work in your profession’s own
  tool plus a report in Docs, marked like real work. You need 80%; if you don’t pass,
  practise and take it again. See [CAREER_GUIDE.md](CAREER_GUIDE.md).

## XP, levels and promotion

Passing work earns **XP** and builds skills. To be promoted you need:

- enough XP;
- every task of your level passed;
- a good enough professional reputation;
- for senior roles, a good weekly review;
- from level 2, your level’s readiness assessment.

The **Career** app shows what you still need, plus your performance record and
achievements. See [CAREER_GUIDE.md](CAREER_GUIDE.md).

## Your portfolio

Everything you pass becomes a portfolio item: what was asked, how it was marked, and
copies of the files you handed in. From Portfolio you can download:

- PDFs of single items;
- a full portfolio PDF;
- a progress report;
- a one-page profile;
- a ZIP with everything.

See [PORTFOLIO_GUIDE.md](PORTFOLIO_GUIDE.md).

## Saving, backups and recovery

- **Saving** is automatic, in this browser on this device. Nothing is uploaded.
- **Backups.** Download one regularly from **Storage & backup**; WorkSim reminds you
  weekly. A backup restores everything, on this or another computer.
- **Restoring** shows what the backup contains first. WorkSim downloads a backup of
  your current data and keeps a **safety copy** on this device, so a restore can be
  undone.
- **Crashes.** If the browser or computer crashes, your work is recovered when you
  reopen WorkSim.

See [PRIVACY_AND_SECURITY.md](PRIVACY_AND_SECURITY.md).

## Installing and offline use

In Chrome or Edge, install WorkSim from the address bar to use it as an app. Once
opened online, it starts and saves offline too. Opening a career for the first time
needs a connection. Updates wait until you choose **Update now**. See
[INSTALL_AND_OFFLINE.md](INSTALL_AND_OFFLINE.md).

## Sound

The office, the street, the café and home each sound different and change through the
day: typing and phones at work, traffic and birds outside, the coffee machine in the
café, the fridge and a clock at home. Footsteps keep time with your walk (road or
pavement), doors open and close, and there are sounds for a new email, a finished
task, pay day, an offer and a promotion. Music plays now and then, and changes with
where you are; an interview has its own.

**Sound & graphics** (in the top bar, or ♪ on the taskbar) has a master volume, a volume and an
on/off switch for music, surroundings, interface and game sounds, your character and
people, and **Mute all sound**. **Fewer sounds** makes everything calmer (it is on at
first if your system asks for less motion). **Sound captions** shows a short note for
important sounds. Each kind has a **Test** button. Sound starts after your first click
or key press, and the game is fully playable without it. Every sound is made on your
computer as you play: nothing is downloaded. See [SOUND_PLAN.md](SOUND_PLAN.md).

**Graphics** (in the same panel): Auto, High or Low detail for the 3D office, home,
street and places. Low has fewer lights and people and is smoother on slower
computers; Auto picks it on computers without a graphics card.

**Weather** (same panel): on some days it rains outdoors, seen and heard; choose
**Always dry** for no rain. **Controls** (same panel): change the key for any
control (choose it, then press the new key), or reset them all.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem                                                    | What to do                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| WorkSim behaves strangely after an update                  | Storage & backup → **Repair app files**. Your saved work is not touched.                       |
| “WorkSim could not load the content for this career”       | Reconnect to the internet (needed the first time a career is opened) and choose **Try again**. |
| “WorkSim is open in another tab”                           | Close the other tab, or choose to use this one. Only one tab can change your work at a time.   |
| “Part of your saved data is damaged”                       | Nothing was changed. Open **Storage & backup** and restore a backup or a safety copy.          |
| Storage is nearly full                                     | Download a backup, then delete old files you no longer need. Storage & backup shows the space. |
| The 3D office is slow                                      | Use the panel beside it; WorkSim also draws more simply on computers without a graphics card.  |
| Progress is missing after using a private/incognito window | Private windows delete their storage when closed. Use a normal window, and keep backups.       |
